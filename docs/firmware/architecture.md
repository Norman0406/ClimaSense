# Firmware architecture

Status: **agreed 2026-11-19**, before any sensor code exists. Framework and language are in [`../decisions.md`](../decisions.md).

## Reading the sensors

The sensors are read **continuously in the background**; publishing takes a snapshot of the latest values. Reading them only when publishing does not work, because of two of the four sensors:

| Sensor | Needs | Schedule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SGP41** (VOC/NOx) | A reading every second, each one compensated with the current T/RH. Sensirion's index algorithm assumes that 1 s rhythm and learns the room's baseline over hours. Conditioning command for the first 10 s after power-up (no longer: it can damage the sensor). | Every 1 s tick, after the SHT45 of the same tick; conditioning for the first 10 ticks after boot |
| **SCD41** (CO2) | Single shot: command, wait 5 s, read. Accepts the ambient pressure for compensation. | Started every 5 minutes, collected 6 ticks later (`SingleShotSchedule`); pressure from the BMP580 |
| **SHT45** (T/RH) | On demand, about 8 ms. | Every tick (the SGP41 needs it); the publisher averages |
| **BMP580** (pressure) | On demand, a few ms. | Every tick, or every few ticks |

**CO2 every 5 minutes, kept on 2026-11-20 after asking whether it could be faster.** The SCD41 can measure every 5 s (periodic, ~15 mA), every 30 s (low-power periodic, ~3.2 mA) or in single shots as often as every 5 s. Single shot every 5 minutes (~0.45 mA) was chosen for accuracy rather than energy: the SCD41 is the largest heat source next to the SHT45, and its automatic self-calibration is tuned for exactly this interval in single-shot mode, so nothing has to be written to its EEPROM. The cost is a slow reaction (an opened window shows up to 5 minutes late). Revisit after step 10 has measured the SHT45's self-heating: 30 s low-power periodic mode is the alternative if faster CO2 values are wanted (about 8 mW more, self-calibration unchanged).

## Tasks

ESP-IDF already runs Wi-Fi, the network stack, the MQTT client and the HTTP server in tasks of their own. The firmware adds:

- **Sensor task.** The only code that touches the sensor I2C bus, so the sensors need no locking among themselves and network delays cannot disturb the SGP41's timing. Runs on a fixed 1 s tick (`vTaskDelayUntil`, no drift). Also owns recovery (step 9, `sensors/recovery.h`): when a sensor has failed 10 readings in a row (the SCD41, measured every 5 minutes: two shots), or the bus could not be opened, it deletes the bus driver, switches the sensor rail off (`SENS_EN`, which also turns SDA/SCL into plain inputs) for 1 s and on again, waits the 1 s start-up time, opens the bus and sets up all four sensors again: the SGP41 gets its 10 s of conditioning, the SCD41 a new shot. One rail powers all four, so a recovery restarts all of them; the first comes at once, each further one after a pause that doubles from 2 minutes to 4 hours, back to 2 minutes once all sensors have worked for an hour, so that a sensor that is broken for good does not disturb the others every few seconds. The gas index algorithms are not reset by a recovery: the gap of about 13 s is short against their time constants of hours, and a reset would throw away the NOx state, which cannot be restored.
- **App task** (`app_main`). Wi-Fi, MQTT, captive portal, OTA, LED status, button events. Publishes on its own interval (decided with the MQTT step).
- **Sensor bus:** on the ESP32-C6's low-power I2C controller (`LP_I2C_NUM_0`), which ESP-IDF's normal I2C master driver supports and which is wired only to IO6/IO7, the sensor pins. 100 kHz, the SCD41's maximum. The task addresses nothing on the bus until 1 s after the rail came on (SCD41 start-up). The drivers see the bus only through `sensors::I2cBus` (probe, write, read, write-then-read), implemented by `EspI2cBus` on the chip and mocked in the unit tests. The normal I2C controller stays free for the Qwiic bus.
- **Sensor drivers** (`Sht45`, `Bmp580`, `Scd41`, `Sgp41`) are plain classes on top of `I2cBus`, without ESP-IDF calls and without waiting: each measurement is split into "start" and "read", and the sensor task does the waiting between them (`vTaskDelay`). That keeps them fully testable with the mock and lets the task decide how to overlap waits. Values are integers in milli-units (milli-degrees, milli-percent), so there is no floating point in the drivers and test values are exact. The three Sensirion sensors share one framing helper (`sensirion.h`: 16-bit words, each with a CRC-8). Short command waits of the SCD41 (1 ms) go through a delay function passed in; everything longer stays with the task.
- **Vendored code:** Sensirion's Gas Index Algorithm (release 3.2.2, the fixed-point variant because the ESP32-C6 has no FPU, BSD-3) is copied unchanged into `components/sensors/vendor/gas_index_algorithm/`, with its licence and a note of version, commit and file hashes, and wrapped for C++ by `gas_index.h`. The VOC algorithm's learned state is kept across software resets (step 9, `sensors/voc_baseline.h`, through `GasIndexAlgorithm_get_states`/`set_states`), within Sensirion's rules: saved only after 3 hours of learning, restored only after an interruption of at most 10 minutes. A copy in RTC memory (magic number and checksum, written every second) survives software resets: updates, crashes, watchdog resets, the restart after saving settings; it is restored at start. After a power cut the algorithm learns again: a copy in NVS that survives one was built and then dropped (2026-11-22) as not worth its code, since power cuts are rare and a restore is only allowed after at most 10 minutes off. A restored state counts as learned, so it is saved again at once. NOx has no saved state in Sensirion's algorithm and learns again after each reboot.
- **Handover:** a `SnapshotBuilder` behind a mutex (`sensors/snapshot.h`). The sensor task adds every reading; the app task calls `take_snapshot()` once per publish interval, which returns the interval's means (temperature, humidity, pressure), the latest gas indices of the interval and the latest CO2 value with its age (up to 11 minutes), and starts the next window. No other shared state.
- **LED and button** run on the LEDC peripheral and a timer, not on tasks of their own.
- **Task watchdog** (step 9): ESP-IDF's task watchdog watches the idle task, the app task and the sensor task, with a 20 s timeout (`sdkconfig.defaults`): longer than the longest legitimate block (a Wi-Fi scan for the portal, retried once, about 8 s; a rail cycle, about 3 s), short enough to catch a hang. A timeout panics: core dump, restart, reported at the next start.

## Components

```
firmware/
  main/                    app_main and top-level wiring
  components/
    ble/                   Bluetooth tag scan: frame parsing and table of tags heard (pure), scanner
    board/                 pin map, supply rails, status LED, button
    diagnostics/           build fingerprint, last log lines across resets, crash dump report
    settings/              settings store: NVS over build defaults, device name
    portal/                setup portal: DNS answers, form, pages, access point and web server
    ota/                   updates: version and command logic, trial guard (pure), download, upload
    sensors/               (step 3/4) bus access and the four drivers
    net/                   Wi-Fi, network time, settings record (step 5); MQTT, portal, OTA later
  test/host/               tests that run on the PC
```

Each component has its public headers in `include/<component>/` (included as `"board/led.h"`) and its sources and private headers in `src/`. Dependencies between components are declared in their `CMakeLists.txt`, so the build enforces the layering.

## Testing without hardware

Logic that does not touch hardware lives in headers and sources without ESP-IDF includes (for example `button_logic.h`, `led_logic.h`). `firmware/test/host/` compiles those for the PC and checks them with GoogleTest unit tests: press detection, LED colour balance, and later the sensor CRCs, conversions and command/response parsing, against the examples in the datasheets. The sensor drivers are written against a small I2C interface of our own, so the unit tests can check the bytes a driver sends and replay datasheet responses through a GoogleMock mock of that interface. GitHub Actions runs the unit tests and the ESP-IDF build on every pull request and every push to `main` that touches `firmware/`.

Code that does touch hardware (LEDC duty on the pins, timers, the I2C driver against real sensors) can only be tested on a board; ESP-IDF's on-target test framework (Unity, run with `pytest-embedded`) is the tool for that once a board is at hand.

The host tests need a C++ compiler for the PC; on this machine that is g++ in WSL (`sudo apt install g++ cmake ninja-build`). Steps are in `firmware/README.md`.

Until the boards arrive there are no sensors to test against (no breakouts for these parts), and the dev board's model is not known yet; a board variant for it will be added once it is.

## Status LED and button

- **Status patterns** (step 9, `board/status_pattern.h`, pure and tested): the app task picks one status by priority (updating, setup open, no Wi-Fi, no broker, sensor fault, all well) and plays its pattern from the start whenever the status changes, waking exactly when the LED next changes. Faults are short flashes (150 ms every 3 s, at most 10 % lit): little light in a dark room and no heat next to the sensors. All well is dark; a short press shows green for 5 s. Each state message lights the LED green over "all well" and "sensor fault" from when it is queued until the broker's ack arrives (QoS 1), at least 80 ms so it is visible, at most 5 s; a flash longer than a blink means a slow link or broker. The setting `status_led` in the device settings (portal checkbox "Status LED", on by default, NVS key `status_led`) switches the LED off, e.g. for a bedroom: then only the setup portal and a short press light it, and the colour check at start is skipped, so a restart at night stays dark. The table is in `firmware/README.md`, "Status LED". The short press used to cycle through the colours for the step-2 colour check; the check at start (red, green, blue, white) stays.
- **LED:** three LEDC PWM channels at 4 kHz, 10 bit, output inverted in the peripheral (the LED is active low, so duty 0 = off). Colours are gamma-corrected and each channel is scaled so that equal values look about equally bright. The scale factors come from the datasheet estimate in `docs/hardware/schematic.md` (green ~21 mcd, red ~13 mcd, blue ~8 mcd at full current, ±30 %) and are to be checked by eye on the real board.
- **Button** (BOOT/user, IO9, low when pressed): polled every 10 ms, debounced over 30 ms. A press released before 3 s is a **short press**; holding it for 3 s gives a **long press** at that moment (the release afterwards gives nothing), so the LED can confirm while the button is still held. Long press opens the Wi-Fi setup portal (step 7); short press closes it, or shows the status. Holding BOOT while pressing RESET still enters USB download mode; that happens in the chip's ROM, before the firmware runs.

## Network and settings

- **Settings are records, the build only provides defaults.** The Wi-Fi code gets a `net::NetSettings` (network name, password, country, time server, time zone; fixed-size, no heap) and never reads build settings itself. `net::default_settings()` fills it from the Kconfig menu "ClimaSense network", whose values come from the git-ignored `firmware/sdkconfig.defaults.local`. From step 7 on, the captive portal stores settings in NVS, which take precedence; it checks them with `net::validate()` first. The MQTT settings in step 6 follow the same pattern.
- **Settings store** (`components/settings`): each settings record is loaded at start from NVS (namespace `settings`, one entry per field, so fields can be added without migration) over the build defaults. A stored field wins even when empty; a stored set that fails validation, or a value too long for its field, is ignored as a whole and the build defaults are used, with a log line saying so. Saving validates first and writes nothing if anything is invalid. Load and save are plain logic against a small `KvStore` interface, tested with an in-memory store; `NvsStore` is the chip's implementation. Records so far: `net::NetSettings` and `settings::DeviceSettings` (the device name and the status LED switch).
- **Captive portal** (`components/portal`, step 7): opens on first boot (no network configured) or on a 3 s button press; a short press closes it, and so does a 10-minute timer. The station keeps running next to an open access point `ClimaSense-<id>` (APSTA, on the station's channel), and its reconnect attempts pause meanwhile so it does not leave that channel. A DNS task answers every A query with the device's address (other types get an empty answer, so clients use IPv4; TTL 60 s), DHCP announces the portal address (option 114, RFC 8910), and the web server redirects every unknown path, including the portal checks of Android, iOS and Windows, to the page with a `303` and a body (iOS needs the body). The page lists nearby networks (scanned when setup opens and on request), and the device name, country, time server and time zone; saving checks the form with the same rules (`apply_form`), stores it through the settings store and restarts. An empty password field keeps the stored password for the same network; a new network needs its password or "open network" ticked. Passwords are never written into a page; everything from outside (scanned names, the user's input) is HTML-escaped. DNS answers, form parsing and checks, pages and the network list are pure and unit-tested; the access point, sockets and web server are not testable without a board. The LED blinks blue while setup is open.
- **Wi-Fi station** (`net/wifi.h`): starts in the background from `app_main` after NVS is initialised; with no network configured it stays off and says so. Country `CH` (channels 1-13), not taken over from the access point. WPA2, and WPA3 where the router offers it; all channels scanned, the strongest access point chosen. Reconnects with pauses of 1, 2, 4 ... 60 s (`net/backoff.h`), back to 1 s once connected. Step 9 closed three gaps: an attempt the driver refuses to start (it returns an error and sends no disconnect event, for example while scanning for the portal) is scheduled again instead of ending the chain; losing the IP address while still associated (`IP_EVENT_STA_LOST_IP`, a DHCP lease not renewed) leaves the network, so the disconnect starts a fresh connection; and opening the portal no longer drops a reconnect that was already scheduled. Power save stays at ESP-IDF's default (modem sleep) for less heat. Host name `climasense-` plus the last three MAC bytes (`net/hostname.h`).
- **Network time:** SNTP from `ch.pool.ntp.org`, started at the first connection and resyncing on its own; time zone Europe/Zurich with daylight saving time. The log keeps its milliseconds-since-boot timestamps; real time is for the MQTT messages.

## MQTT

- **Client** (`components/telemetry`, on `espressif/mqtt`): plain TCP to the broker in the settings, client ID = device ID, keepalive 60 s, last will `offline` (retained, QoS 1) on `<base>/<id>/status`. The client reconnects on its own every 10 s (esp-mqtt's default). Our own backoff with an immediate retry when Wi-Fi gets an address was built in step 9 and dropped again (2026-11-22): it saved at most 10 s after an outage. A connection error is logged once per outage (esp-mqtt still logs each failed attempt itself). On every connection: `online` (retained), and with Home Assistant discovery on, one retained config message per value to `<prefix>/sensor/<id>/<field>/config`, again whenever Home Assistant announces `online` on `<prefix>/status`.
- **State message** every 60 s from the app task (the first after a full minute), queued without waiting, QoS 1 (since 2026-11-22, so that the status LED can show when the broker has the values; a value can rarely arrive twice or up to 30 s late after a reconnect, which a gauge in Prometheus does not mind), not retained, dropped while disconnected: `{"id","name","time","temperature_c","humidity_pct","pressure_hpa","co2_ppm","co2_age_s","voc_index","nox_index","rssi_dbm","uptime_s"}`, numbers as JSON numbers (two decimals for the means), a field left out when there is no value, `time` (RFC 3339, local) only once the clock is set. Built by pure functions (`telemetry/payload.h`), tested byte for byte.
- **Settings:** `telemetry::MqttSettings` (broker, port, user, password, base topic, Home Assistant switch and prefix), with build defaults in the Kconfig menu "ClimaSense MQTT", stored and edited like the others; the portal's MQTT section sends a hidden marker so that an unticked checkbox means "off".

- **Offline restart** (step 9, `net/offline_guard.h`): after an hour without the connection the device is set up for (the broker if one is set, else Wi-Fi; the same rule as the update trial), the app task logs an error and restarts. It is the last resort against a network stack or driver that has stopped trying; it does not run while the portal is open or an update runs. A device whose router stays off restarts once an hour, which costs nothing: there is nowhere to send values, and the VOC baseline survives. The reason is in the boot report's log lines afterwards.

## Bluetooth tag scan

Added 2026-11-23 for presence detection ([`../research/presence-sensing.md`](../research/presence-sensing.md)). **Built, not run; nothing measured.** The boards only collect and forward; recognising whose tag an identifier belongs to needs the tag's key and happens in a service on the network, not in the firmware.

- **Scanner** (`components/ble`, on ESP-IDF's NimBLE host with only the observer role built in): a passive scan, legacy advertisements only, 300 ms of every 1000 ms (`ble/scanner.h`). It never transmits. The 30 % were chosen on 2026-11-23 between reception and heat, on estimates: a tag sends its frame at least every 2 s, so it is heard after about 6 s on average, for about 42 mW more; 10 % would be about 20 s and 14 mW, listening all the time about 1 s and 140 mW, next to sensors that were brought down to 12 mW. Wi-Fi stays connected: in modem sleep it needs the radio only for the router's beacons and for sending. To be checked on a board, the SHT45's reading above all.
- **Filter** (`ble/fhn_frame.h`, pure): of every advertisement received, only service data for UUID `0xFEAA` with frame type `0x40` or `0x41` and a 20- or 32-byte identifier is kept. The controller's duplicate filter is off: it works by address, and every frame counts as a sign of life.
- **Table** (`ble/sightings.h`, pure): 24 entries, no heap. Per identifier the strongest signal, the frame count and the time last heard in the current window; an identifier not heard for 2 minutes is forgotten, so hearing it again counts as new. With the table full, the entry heard longest ago makes room, unless all were heard in this window: then the frame is counted as dropped.
- **Tasks:** NimBLE's host task calls the scanner for every advertisement; table access is behind a mutex. The app task takes a report once per interval and builds the message (`telemetry::ble_json`). The report (about 1 KB) is a static, not on the app task's stack.
- **Message** on `<base>/<id>/ble`, QoS 0, not retained: every 60 s, also when empty, and 2 s after a new identifier was heard (checked once a second). Its own topic, because the state message is flat numbers for Prometheus. Format in `firmware/README.md`.
- **Paused** while the setup portal is open (Espressif rates access point plus Bluetooth scan as unstable) and during an update. While paused nothing is published, so a board that is not listening does not look like one that heard no tag; the window restarts when the scan resumes.
- **Setting:** `tag_scan` in `settings::DeviceSettings` (portal checkbox "Bluetooth tag scan", NVS key `tag_scan`), off by default. The Bluetooth stack is only started when it is on and a broker is configured; it is always built in, which costs about 280 KB of flash (237 KB of it Espressif's controller) and about 29 KB of RAM at build time; the image is 1.64 MB in a 3 MB slot, so no partition change. The heap the stack takes when started is not measured.

## Updates over the air

- **Signed images only** (`sdkconfig.defaults`): ESP-IDF's signed apps without hardware Secure Boot, RSA-3072. The running firmware's signature block holds the public key; an update is checked against it when it is finished (`esp_https_ota_finish`, `esp_ota_end`), so an image not signed with our key never becomes bootable, however it arrived. Only release images are signed: every build is unsigned (`CONFIG_SECURE_BOOT_BUILD_SIGNED_BINARIES=n`), and the release workflow (`.github/workflows/release.yml`, on a pushed tag vX.Y.Z or started by hand on `main` with a version, which then creates the tag on the built commit; with the key from the repository secret `OTA_SIGNING_KEY`) signs it with `espsecure sign-data`, verifies it and publishes it as a GitHub Release. A board runs unsigned development builds normally but accepts updates only once a signed release has been flashed onto it.
- **From a URL** (`ota::start_from_url`): an MQTT message on `<base>/<id>/set/ota` with `<url>` or `<url> force` (retained commands are ignored, or every restart would update again); a task of its own downloads with `esp_https_ota` over HTTP or HTTPS, refuses an older or non-`x.y.z` version unless forced, reports `{"state","progress","detail"}` on `<base>/<id>/ota` (starting, downloading in 10 % steps, refused, failed, restarting) and restarts.
- **By upload** in the setup portal (`ota::Upload`, `POST /update`): the page's script sends the file as the request body; the version is read from the image's first 80 bytes before anything is written (`ota::image_version`), then the image is written to the free slot and checked.
- **Trial and rollback**: after an update the new firmware starts in `ESP_OTA_IMG_PENDING_VERIFY`. `app_main` asks `ota::TrialGuard` once a second: accepted once healthy (connected to the broker if one is set, else to Wi-Fi if a network is set) after at least 3 minutes; rejected after 10 minutes otherwise, which marks it invalid and restarts into the previous firmware. A crash or reset during the trial also returns to the previous firmware (the bootloader does that). The boot report says `"on_trial":true`. No new update is accepted while on trial.
- **Version** (`firmware/cmake/version.cmake`, as `PROJECT_VER`): `X.Y.Z+<hash>` for a commit tagged vX.Y.Z, `<branch>-<hash>` otherwise (`-dirty` with uncommitted changes under `firmware/`), `unknown` without git; in GitHub Actions from `GITHUB_REF_TYPE`/`GITHUB_REF_NAME`/`GITHUB_SHA`. At most 31 characters (the branch name is shortened). `ota::parse_version` ignores the `+<hash>`, so releases compare by number; branch versions are not comparable. The release workflow checks that the built image carries `X.Y.Z+<hash>` before signing it.

## Debugging

Decided 2026-11-20. The device hangs on a USB charger, so nothing reads its USB console; what happened before a reset has to survive the reset.

- **Logging:** ESP-IDF's `ESP_LOGx` with one tag per module, INFO and above, milliseconds since boot. Output goes to the native USB console (`idf.py monitor`).
- **Last log lines across a reset:** every log line also goes into a 2 KB ring buffer in RTC memory (`RTC_NOINIT_ATTR`), which keeps its content across a software reset, panic or watchdog reset, but not a power cut. At the next start the firmware prints them as "last ... bytes of log before the reset" and keeps a copy for publishing. After a power-on or brownout the buffer is discarded. The log hook only formats the line and copies it in a critical section; it never sends anything, so it cannot deadlock inside Wi-Fi or MQTT code. Lines longer than 160 characters are kept shortened.
- **Crash dumps:** a panic (exception, failed assert or `ESP_ERROR_CHECK`, interrupt watchdog, task watchdog, which is set to panic and watches the app and sensor tasks with 20 s) writes a core dump to the `coredump` partition (128 KB) and reboots. At the next start the firmware logs a summary: panic reason, task, PC, RISC-V cause registers, and the fingerprint of the build that crashed. The dump stays in flash until it has been reported (step 6 erases it after publishing); a later crash overwrites it.
- **Build fingerprint:** 16 hex characters of the app's ELF SHA-256, in the start-up banner and in every core dump. A backtrace or core dump decodes only with the `.elf` of exactly that build: the local `firmware/build/climasense.elf` for a build flashed from the PC, or the `firmware-<commit>` artifact that CI keeps for 90 days.
- **Partition table** (`firmware/partitions.csv`): NVS 24 KB, OTA data, PHY data, two 3 MB OTA app slots, core dump 128 KB, 1.75 MB free. Changing it on a deployed board needs a USB flash, so it already has what OTA (step 8) needs.

Over MQTT (step 6b):

- **Boot report** on `<base>/<id>/boot`, once per start, QoS 1 and retained: ID, name, firmware, build, reset reason, the log lines kept from before the reset (up to 2 KB) and, if the coredump partition holds one, the crash summary (reason, task, PC and RISC-V registers as hex, the crashed build and whether it is this one). Sent on the first connection and again on later ones until the broker acknowledges it; only then is the core dump erased. The client's buffer is 4 KB for it.
- **Log lines** on `<base>/<id>/log`, WARNING and above by default: the log hook also copies each line it would forward into a 16-line queue (`diagnostics/line_queue.h`; when full, new lines are dropped and counted), and the app task sends them once a second while connected, as `{"level","tag","t_ms","msg"}`, at most 10 a minute (`telemetry/rate_limit.h`); what did not go out is reported in one note with its count. Lines from the MQTT code itself (`mqtt`, `mqtt_client`, `transport_base`) are never forwarded, so a broken connection cannot feed itself. Log text is made valid UTF-8 before it goes into JSON.
- **Remote log level:** `none`, `error`, `warn`, `info` or `debug` sent to `<base>/<id>/set/log_level` sets which lines are forwarded, until the next restart (then WARNING again). It does not change what the console shows.

Optional later: upload the full core dump over HTTP, or forward the log topic to a log service from the broker side.

On the desk, the C6's USB port is also a JTAG debugger: `idf.py openocd` and `idf.py gdb` give breakpoints and single-stepping over the same cable.
